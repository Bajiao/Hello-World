## Slide 4: Early Computing Era (Ancient Times - 1940)

### Major Innovations:

**🔵 Abacus (c. 2400 BCE)** - First calculating tool  
**🔵 Pascaline (1642)** - Blaise Pascal's mechanical calculator  
**🔵 Analytical Engine (1837)** - Charles Babbage's computer design  
**🔴 Ada Lovelace (1843)** - First computer programmer  
**🟣 Boolean Algebra (1854)** - George Boole's logical foundation  

### Key Discovery: 
**The Antikythera Mechanism (100 BCE)** - Ancient Greek analog computer that predicted astronomical positions

*Images: Abacus, Pascaline photo, Babbage Engine model, Ada Lovelace portrait*

---

## Slide 5: Electronic Age (1940-1970)

### Revolutionary Breakthroughs:

**🟣 ENIAC (1946)** - First general-purpose electronic computer  
**🟣 Transistor (1947)** - Replaced vacuum tubes, enabled miniaturization  
**🔵 UNIVAC I (1951)** - First commercial computer  
**🔵 IBM System/360 (1964)** - Compatible computer family  
**🟣 ARPANET (1969)** - Internet's predecessor  

### Key Discovery: 
**Manchester Baby (1948)** - First stored-program computer, ran its first program on June 21, 1948

**Impact:** Computers evolved from room-sized machines to business tools

*Images: ENIAC operators, Transistor comparison, UNIVAC predicting election*

---

## Slide 6: Personal Computer Era (1970-1990)

### Making Computers Personal:

**🟣 Intel 4004 (1971)** - First microprocessor  
**🔵 Altair 8800 (1975)** - First personal computer kit  
**🔵 Apple II (1977)** - First successful home computer  
**🔵 IBM PC (1981)** - Established PC standard  
**🟣 World Wide Web (1989-1991)** - Tim Berners-Lee's innovation  

### Key Discovery: 
**Xerox Alto (1973)** - First computer with graphical user interface, mouse, and ethernet networking

**Impact:** Computers moved from businesses to homes and schools

*Images: Intel 4004 chip, Apple II in classroom, IBM PC advertisement*

## Slide 9: Most Interesting Discovery

### My Most Fascinating Discovery:
**Grace Hopper's "Bug" Story (1947)**

### What Made It Interesting:
• **Surprising Detail:** The first computer "bug" was literally a moth stuck in a relay  
• **Historical Importance:** Led to the term "debugging" we still use today  
• **Modern Connection:** Every programmer today "debugs" their code  

### Personal Reflection:
*"This discovery changed my understanding of programming terminology because it showed how even our everyday words have fascinating historical origins."*

### Fun Fact:
Grace Hopper kept the actual moth taped in her logbook, which is now in the Smithsonian!

*Images: Grace Hopper, the actual moth logbook page, Harvard Mark II computer*

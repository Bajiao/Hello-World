# Lesson 1: History of Computing

## Learning Objectives
By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
- Identify key milestones in computing history
- Understand the evolution from mechanical to electronic computers
- Explain the significance of early computing pioneers
- Describe how computing has transformed society

## Prerequisites
- Basic understanding of what a computer is
- Curiosity about technology and history

## Materials Needed
- Computer with internet access
- Notebook for taking notes
- Access to video resources

## Lesson Overview (45 minutes)
1. **Introduction** (5 minutes) - What is computing?
2. **Early Computing Devices** (15 minutes) - Mechanical calculators
3. **Electronic Revolution** (15 minutes) - First computers
4. **Personal Computer Era** (8 minutes) - Making computers accessible
5. **Wrap-up and Discussion** (2 minutes)

---

## Detailed Instructions

### 1. Introduction: What is Computing? (5 minutes)

**Discussion Starter:**
- "What do you think was the first computer ever made?"
- "How do you think people did calculations before computers?"

**Key Points:**
- Computing = performing calculations and processing information
- Humans have always needed tools to help with complex calculations
- Computing devices have evolved from simple tools to powerful machines

### 2. Early Computing Devices (15 minutes)

#### The Abacus (c. 2400 BCE)
- **What it was:** Ancient calculating tool using beads on rods
- **Significance:** First mechanical aid for calculation
- **Still used today:** In some parts of Asia for education

**Video Resource:** [History of the Abacus](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1_YfmcOgRIk) (3 minutes)

#### Pascaline (1642)
- **Creator:** Blaise Pascal
- **Purpose:** Mechanical calculator for his father's tax work
- **Innovation:** First mechanical calculator that could carry digits

#### Analytical Engine (1837)
- **Creator:** Charles Babbage
- **Significance:** First design for a general-purpose computer
- **Features:** Had all elements of a modern computer (input, output, memory, CPU)
- **Problem:** Technology of the time couldn't build it reliably

**Key Figure: Ada Lovelace**
- Wrote the first computer program for the Analytical Engine
- Recognized that computers could do more than just math
- Often called the "first programmer"

**Video Resource:** [Charles Babbage and Ada Lovelace](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BlbQsKpq3Ak) (10 minutes)

### 3. Electronic Revolution (15 minutes)

#### ENIAC (1946)
- **Full Name:** Electronic Numerical Integrator and Computer
- **Size:** Filled entire room, weighed 30 tons
- **Purpose:** Calculate artillery firing tables for the military
- **Speed:** 1,000 times faster than manual calculations

**Fun Fact:** Programming ENIAC required physically rewiring the machine!

#### UNIVAC I (1951)
- **Breakthrough:** First commercial computer
- **Famous moment:** Correctly predicted Eisenhower's 1952 election victory on live TV
- **Impact:** Showed the public what computers could do

**Video Resource:** [ENIAC - The First Computer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k4oGI_dNaPc) (8 minutes)

#### IBM Mainframes (1960s)
- **IBM System/360:** First compatible family of computers
- **Business Impact:** Companies could upgrade without rewriting software
- **Legacy:** IBM became synonymous with business computing

### 4. Personal Computer Era (8 minutes)

#### The Shift to Personal Computing
- **1970s:** Computers were room-sized and cost millions
- **Goal:** Make computers small and affordable for individuals

#### Apple II (1977)
- **Innovation:** First successful personal computer
- **Features:** Color graphics, sound, easy to use
- **Impact:** Brought computers to homes and schools

**Video Resource:** [Apple II Introduction](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jPn2hZvpLTc) (5 minutes)

#### IBM PC (1981)
- **Strategy:** Used standard parts, open architecture
- **Result:** Other companies could make compatible computers
- **Legacy:** Established the PC standard still used today

#### The Internet Connection
- **ARPANET (1969):** First network connecting computers
- **World Wide Web (1991):** Made internet user-friendly
- **Impact:** Connected all computers globally

**Video Resource:** [History of the Internet](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9hIQjrMHTv4) (8 minutes)

### 5. Wrap-up and Discussion (2 minutes)

**Key Takeaways:**
- Computing evolved from mechanical tools to electronic machines
- Each generation solved problems and created new possibilities
- Personal computers democratized access to computing power
- The internet connected the world's computers

**Discussion Questions:**
- How has computing changed since you were born?
- What do you think the next major breakthrough will be?
- How might computing continue to change our world?

## Activities

### Individual Reflection (5 minutes)
Write a short paragraph answering: "Which computing breakthrough do you think had the biggest impact on society and why?"

### Timeline Creation (Starting point for activity)
Begin creating a timeline that will be completed in the activity session. Include:
- Major devices/inventions
- Key people
- Important dates
- Impact on society

---

## Assessment

### Formative Assessment (During Lesson)
- **Participation:** Active engagement in discussions
- **Questions:** Thoughtful questions about the content
- **Connections:** Ability to connect past innovations to present technology

### Exit Ticket (End of Lesson)
Answer these questions before leaving:
1. Name one early computing device and its purpose
2. Who is considered the first programmer?
3. What made personal computers different from earlier computers?

## Extensions

### For Advanced Students
- Research one computing pioneer not mentioned in class
- Compare the capabilities of ENIAC to a modern smartphone
- Investigate how computing developed differently in other countries

### For Struggling Students
- Focus on the timeline activity with visual aids
- Use analogies to connect computing concepts to familiar objects
- Provide additional videos with simpler explanations
